There was no countdown livestream. No subscriber milestone party. On January 22, 2026, the DEL Records YouTube channel crossed **12 million subscribers** the same way it built every one of the 11,999,999 before it: someone pressed play on a music video, and then another person pressed play, and then another, and then another, **11 billion times** over the course of **17 years**.

The number is **12,007,013**, to be exact. The total view count sits at **11 billion**. The cumulative watch time is **478.6 million hours** - roughly 54,600 years of continuous playback. Since the channel launched on January 30, 2009, DEL Records has quietly built what is now the largest regional Mexican music label channel on YouTube and one of the most-subscribed independent label channels in the world.

> What it means: DEL Records' 12 million subscriber milestone did not come from a single viral moment. It came from 17 years of catalog-driven growth - 389 music videos, three videos past 750 million views, and a playlist strategy that drives 69% of all traffic. The channel pulls 58.9 million views every 28 days.

The achievement lands during the most significant growth period regional Mexican music has ever experienced. According to Billboard, regional Mexican was the second-largest growth genre in the United States during the first half of 2025, expanding **32.95%** year over year. On Spotify, streams of música Mexicana grew over **440%** globally between 2018 and 2023. Amazon Music reported the genre grew **249%** year over year in 2024, making it the number one Latin category on the platform.

But DEL Records was not riding the wave. It helped build it.

## How Did DEL Records Get to 11 Billion Views?

The answer is not complicated, though it is unusual by music industry standards: full-length music videos, organized into playlists, consumed in long sessions by an audience that comes back.

Of the channel's 11 billion total views, traditional long-form music videos account for **6.78 billion**, representing **99.8%** of all video-driven traffic. DEL Records has published 891 Shorts, but they account for just **0.2%** of views. The audience is not here for clips. They are here for songs.

That shows up in the traffic data. Over the most recent 28-day period ending January 22, 2026, playlists drove **40.6 million views** - **69%** of all traffic to the channel. YouTube search contributed another **6.4 million views** at **10.9%**. People are not stumbling onto this content. They are actively seeking it out and consuming it the way they would an album: track by track, in sequence, for extended sessions.

> Citation: YouTube Studio analytics for the 28-day period ending January 22, 2026, show playlists driving 40.6 million views (69% of total traffic), with YouTube Search adding 6.4 million views (10.9%). (Source: DEL Records YouTube Studio, Feb 2026)

The channel consistently pulls **58.9 million views** and **2.55 million hours** of watch time every 28 days. That is approximately **2.1 million views per day**. The monthly unique audience sits at **18.3 million viewers**, split across new viewers (31.6%), casual viewers (40.6%), and regular returning viewers (27.8%).

Nearly one in three monthly viewers has never watched a DEL Records video before. The catalog is not just holding an audience. It is still building one, 17 years in.

## Which Videos Crossed 500 Million Views?

Five videos on the channel have passed **500 million views**. Three have passed **750 million**. The full top ten reads like a timeline of the biggest moments in regional Mexican music over the last decade.

*No Lo Hice Bien* by Los Plebes del Rancho de Ariel Camacho sits at the top with **764.8 million views**. Released on February 14, 2016, a year after Ariel Camacho's death, it carries a **77.4%** average view duration - meaning three of every four viewers still watch the entire video, a full decade after publication. That kind of retention on a ten-year-old video is nearly unheard of on the platform.

*Yo Ya No Vuelvo Contigo* by Lenin Ramírez featuring Grupo Firme follows at **759.3 million views** (77.9% average view duration). Released November 1, 2019, the collaboration between two DEL Records artists became one of the defining songs of the Regional Mexican streaming era. It reached number one on Billboard's Mexico Popular Airplay chart and earned a Billboard Latin Music Award nomination for Collaboration of the Year.

In third place, at **751.4 million views**, sits the video that changed everything - not just for DEL Records, but for the entire genre.

## What Made "Ella Baila Sola" a Turning Point for the Genre?

When Eslabon Armado and Peso Pluma released *Ella Baila Sola* on April 7, 2023, through DEL Records, regional Mexican music had never reached the mainstream American charts. No song from the genre had cracked the top ten of the Billboard Hot 100. None had topped the Global 200. That all ended in a matter of weeks.

*Ella Baila Sola* peaked at **number 4 on the Billboard Hot 100**, becoming the first regional Mexican song to enter the top ten. It spent **six consecutive weeks at number one** on the Billboard Global 200, another first for the genre. It held the top position on Hot Latin Songs for **19 consecutive weeks**. Spotify crowned it the Global Song of the Summer for 2023 after it accumulated **367 million streams** between late May and mid-August.

> Citation: According to Billboard, "Ella Baila Sola" was the first regional Mexican song to enter the top 10 of the Billboard Hot 100 and the first to top the Billboard Global 200, marking a watershed moment for the genre's mainstream crossover. (Source: Billboard, 2023)

It has since crossed **one billion streams on Spotify** - the first regional Mexican song in history to reach that threshold. The RIAA certified it **21 times Platinum** in the Latin field. Rolling Stone placed it at **number 402** on its updated "500 Greatest Songs of All Time" list and named it the best song of 2023.

Peso Pluma, whose career was launched in large part by that single, went on to win the Grammy Award for Best Música Mexicana Album for *GÉNESIS* at the 66th Annual Grammy Awards in February 2024. He placed **25 simultaneous titles** on the Hot Latin Songs chart, breaking Bad Bunny's previous record of 24. He is currently Mexico's most-streamed artist of all time.

All of this traced back to a song released on DEL Records.

The rest of the channel's top ten confirms the pattern of catalog longevity. *Del Negociante* by Los Plebes del Rancho (611.5 million views, released January 2016), *Porque Me Enamore* by Ulices Chaidez y Sus Plebes (504.7 million, August 2016), *Que Caro Estoy Pagando* by Los Plebes del Rancho (485.3 million, January 2016), *Recordando A Manuel* by Lenin Ramírez featuring Gerardo Ortiz and Jesús Ojeda (409.2 million, May 2017), *Te Regaló* by Ulices Chaidez (408.5 million, January 2017), *Aerolinea Carrillo* by T3R Elemento and Gerardo Ortiz (329.1 million, September 2018), and *Amor Fugaz* by Los Del Limit (296.2 million, May 2021).

The average view duration across these top ten videos ranges from **72.3% to 97.5%**. *Amor Fugaz* holds the highest retention at **97.5%**, meaning virtually every viewer watches the entire three-minute video. These are not songs people click on and scroll past. These are songs people sit with.

## What Is the Story Behind DEL Records and Regional Mexican Music's Rise?

To understand what 12 million subscribers actually represents, you have to go back to 2009.

DEL Records was founded that year by Angel del Villar in Bell Gardens, California, a small city in the southeast corner of Los Angeles County. The label started with a single mission: give regional Mexican artists a home where the music came first. The first major signing was Gerardo Ortiz in 2010, whose album *Morir y Existir* reached number one on Billboard's Latin Albums chart.

Then came Ariel Camacho.

Camacho signed to DEL Records in 2014 with Los Plebes del Rancho, carrying a sierreño sound from Northwestern Mexico that had barely registered outside of Sinaloa. Under the label's promotion, the group built a United States audience that turned a regional style into something much larger. When Camacho died in a car accident on February 25, 2015, at age 22, the loss was devastating. But his catalog exploded. *El Karma* reached number one on Billboard Hot Latin Songs. The posthumous album reached the top five on Latin Albums.

> Why it matters: Ten years after Ariel Camacho's passing, "No Lo Hice Bien" sits at 764.8 million YouTube views - still the most-watched video on the entire DEL Records channel. Artists including Peso Pluma, Christian Nodal, and Fuerza Regida have publicly credited Camacho with paving the way for the current generation. (Source: Billboard, 2024)

The years between 2015 and 2019 built the foundation. Ulices Chaidez's *Porque Me Enamore* hit number one on Billboard's Regional Mexican chart. T3R Elemento brought a younger audience through corridos verdes. Lenin Ramírez's collaborations with Grupo Firme crossed 750 million views. By the time Eslabon Armado's *Nostalgia* debuted at number 9 on the Billboard 200 in 2022 - the first regional Mexican album in the top ten of the main chart - DEL Records had been building quietly for thirteen years.

In 2016, the label received **six Billboard Latin Music Awards**, including Regional Mexican Airplay Label of the Year and Hot Latin Songs Label of the Year. A global partnership with BMG extended its distribution worldwide. The operation grew from recordings into publishing, studios, and live entertainment.

Then came 2023, and *Ella Baila Sola*, and the rest is the history described above.

The label is now led by CEO Andrew del Villar, who was profiled by Billboard in late 2025 as he took the helm. Under his leadership, DEL Records artists continue to perform over **200 live shows annually** across the United States and Mexico.

## Why Does Playlist Traffic Tell the Real Story?

The most revealing number in the entire dataset is not the subscriber count or the total views. It is the **69%**.

That is the share of DEL Records' YouTube traffic that comes from playlists. In the most recent 28-day period, playlists generated **40.6 million views**. To put that in context, YouTube search - the second-largest source - contributed 6.4 million. Suggested videos, external sources, end screens, Shorts feed, and everything else combined did not match what playlists alone produced.

This is not how most YouTube channels work. The typical label channel depends on suggested videos and Browse features (the home feed). DEL Records' audience has built a different habit. They open a playlist - whether it is an official DEL Records collection, a user-generated compilation, or an algorithmically generated mix - and they listen for extended periods.

That behavior explains why the channel's watch time numbers are so high relative to its view count. When your audience watches in sessions rather than individual clicks, every view carries more weight. YouTube's algorithm rewards watch time, which means playlist-driven consumption creates a compounding effect: longer sessions lead to stronger algorithmic signals, which lead to better placement in recommendations, which lead to more playlist sessions.

It also explains how catalog content stays alive. A song from 2016 does not need a new promotional push to generate views if it lives inside a playlist that people return to regularly. The playlist becomes the promotion.

> Key stat: Of DEL Records' 12 million lifetime subscribers, 6.4 million came from traditional video content. Shorts contributed 35,700. The audience is subscribing because of full-length music, not short-form clips. (Source: YouTube Studio, Feb 2026)

## What Does "Todo Lo Fue" Tell Us About What Comes Next?

The channel is not coasting on legacy catalog. In the 48 hours preceding the 12 million milestone, DEL Records pulled **4.93 million views**. The biggest driver of that traffic was not a classic from 2016. It was *Todo Lo Fue* by Lenin Ramírez - a deep album cut from 2024 that went viral on TikTok in late January 2026 and accumulated over **6.2 million YouTube views** in its first eight days.

The *Todo Lo Fue* phenomenon carried a data point that captures exactly what DEL Records does best: **93.6%** of the video's watch time came from non-subscribers. The song did not just engage the existing fanbase. It introduced Lenin Ramírez - a 15-year veteran of the genre - to millions of people who had never heard of him.

That is the DEL Records model in miniature. Build deep catalogs. Give songs room to find their moment. Trust that 478.6 million hours of watch time is not an accident but the result of an audience that keeps coming back because the music holds up.

Regional Mexican is now the most-streamed Latin music category in the United States, topping charts in **26 states**. Half of the 50 most-listened-to songs on Spotify Mexico in 2024 were by Mexican artists, up from just **14%** five years earlier. The genre's share of the U.S. market expanded **32.95%** in the first half of 2025 alone.

DEL Records started in 2009 with one artist and a YouTube channel. Seventeen years later, that channel has **12 million subscribers**, **11 billion views**, three videos past 750 million, the first regional Mexican song in the Hot 100 top ten, and a catalog deep enough to keep producing viral moments from two-year-old album tracks.

The next milestone, 12 billion total views, is already on pace for sometime in 2026. It will arrive the same way 12 million subscribers did. One play at a time.
